# The card-game series, distilled for a 3D skirmish game

Eleven plain-JS card projects (MegaRobotWar, Starbound Legions, Crystal Wars / Final Fantasy TCG, BreachForge, RIVALFORGE, GRAND LINE, FABFORGE, MANAFORGE, MythosForge, ChampionsForge, plus the Lorcana build) produced a large lessons corpus. [other-projects.md](other-projects.md) lists the projects and one line each; this file keeps only the lessons that transfer to a 3D skirmish like Mancermundo and skips card mechanics (stack, priority, payment, fanning, card rendering, hand layout).

**Sources, latest copy wins** (the CARD-GAME-LESSONS-2..6 files are byte-identical copies across `MANAFORGE`, `BreachForge`, `FleshAndBloodTCG`, `ChampionsForge/docs/lessons`, `MythosForge/docs/lessons`): `CARD-GAME-LESSONS-5.md` (RIVALFORGE, first human playtest), `-6.md` (GRAND LINE, per-card audit), `OVERNIGHT-BUILD-PLAYBOOK.md`, `HANDOFF-MAGIC.md` (MANAFORGE), and the 2026-10-24 digests `lorecannae\kb\LESSONS-DIGEST.md` / `CROSS-PROJECT-LESSONS.md` (which cite those files by section; section tags below such as L5 §5.1 point into them). Items marked **MUST** were repeated across several files, the subject of an owner complaint, or a bug that shipped green.

## 1. How the owner reacts (patterns that repeated on every project)
- "Playable first, not endless tests": RIVALFORGE had a clickable board with an AI before art, sound or a second deck; FABFORGE was playable 33 minutes after `.gitignore` (L5 §3, L6 epilogue).
- **The owner's playtest is the specification.** One playtest on RIVALFORGE changed four core rules (board size 3 to 6, the objective-control rule, the captain's opening, the whole purpose of a second location). Shipping a small first pool (20 heroes, 4 decks) is what made that affordable; a hundred cards authored against an unplayed engine are a hundred to redo (L5 §5.8). Murder Squad and Lorcana both followed this (two teams first, then batches).
- **His design instincts have beaten measurements.** He called a control rule skewed before any data existed (measured +7.3, the largest of four candidates) and uncapped Ultimate charge a future problem before it bit. When he says a rule feels wrong, measure that rule first, not last (L5 §3).
- **Recurring complaints**, each raised on several projects: open information hidden (trash shown as a bare count, "look at 5" showing only takeable cards); the game making his choices (auto-target, auto-pay); prompts that do not say what they are about ("a choice of keeping or re-rolling without knowing what for"); the opponent's turn too fast or unreadable; the AI doing nothing on some turns ("there were rounds where nothing was played by AI, causing a quick loss"); layout overflowing; phases announced where nothing happens (L6 §9.1, MAL §3, L5 §5.1).
- **Fidelity is the product** for a reproduction: "make absolutely sure the cards read and play exactly like the real game"; "I don't want anything playable that contains content that doesn't work correctly" (L3 §2). Real names, recognisable characters, signature features in an original style; he rejected anonymous figures and flat per-colour backdrops. Lorcana (2026-10-24, after the first batch): "Darkwing Duck not being a duck is really bad" -> every card shows its actual character in MGSD proportions with an animation-style direction, concern stated once, owner's call.
- **A setting-appropriate skin.** After the Lorcana build used a Starbound-style sci-fi skin: "I like how starbound legions presented because it was appropriate to the Star Wars universe. This is not Star Wars." The structure and interaction were kept; the look was rethemed within hours. Skin the UI for the setting (for Necromunda: hive-city industrial gothic), keep the layout.
- **Cost-sensitive**: hates wasted tokens and paid renders done twice; does not want to be asked obvious questions or for permission to proceed; wants defaults he can correct in one word; does not want concerns repeated or work silently scaled down (PB §1, L3 §10.1).
- **Wants to see it on the web**: three card projects flipped from private to public Pages at his request; he expects a playable link, not a terminal (MythosForge D6, MANAFORGE D12, BreachForge PLAN).
- **Favourite project: GRAND LINE** ("fantastic"): clickable board vs AI first, printed text on the face, painted art, music, how-to-play.

## 2. Process and cost
- **Question round before he sleeps** (PB §1): only questions where a wrong guess costs rework, two batches of at most four through the multiple-choice UI, recommended option first, plus **one question about feel**; everything else stated as a default list. For Necromunda the rework-costly ones are IP posture (real names vs original), scope of first night (how many gangs), art direction, public vs private.
- **Order of work** (PB §2, ChampionsForge git log shows it literally): `.gitignore` committed alone -> authority docs (rights findings, rules citation index, registers) -> engine spine + sim -> first matchup authored -> rest of the pool -> AI -> interface (the playable milestone) -> art -> sound -> handoff. **Every arrow is a commit that runs.**
- **Say up front what gets cut if the night runs short** and what never gets cut. Lorcana: cut tutorial depth, spectate controls, deck-builder polish, music tiers; never cut per-card tests, the defects gate, open-information viewers, the log, prompts that say what they are about.
- **Commit at checkpoints.** A long run that hits a context or usage wall loses everything uncommitted (two `WIP (agents stopped)` commits in the series). Commit messages describe the mechanism by id, so history reads as a bug diary and handoffs become writable.
- **One file owns each decision** (`PLAN.md` decision table with date and `by: owner/default`), and a dated Status rewritten each session. BreachForge shipped three files disagreeing about deployment within three days; when a decision moves, grep the repo for every file restating it. `ls` every path a document claims before committing it (an auditor documented as existing was not written for two days).
- **Registers with a header saying what each is not:** `DEVIATIONS.md` (rules not yet kept, naming the rule; plus a "Not deviations" list), `TODO.md` ("what exists today / constraints / done when"), `data/defects.js` (content that does not play as it reads cannot reach the player; built on day one, empty and wired), `BALANCE.md` only for an original design. The 3D family's equivalent of the first is the RULING log.
- **Morning summary block** at the top of `HANDOFF.md` (what shipped, URLs, screenshot paths, numbered questions in priority order); Dead of Winter's `OWNER-REVIEW.md` checkbox list is the best template seen. Lorcana's HANDOFF followed it: play link, what shipped, five numbered owner questions, not-done list, where things are, how a rule flows, gates, GPU etiquette, open items.
- **Agents.** The card series ran a single agent by default and split bulk content across worktrees by disjoint id ranges with disjoint files. Helpers run on Sonnet (not Haiku), own an explicit file list and do not commit; the lead reviews and re-runs gates. **Independent reviewers beat self-tests**: Lorcana's per-card tests all passed, then two fresh read-only reviewers (cards vs print; engine vs rules) still found 3 + 4 wrong behaviours. Run them as soon as content lands. Authoring guide with worked examples in the real files made 112 card scripts across three agents read as one hand; the engine contract written first (`docs/ENGINE-API.md`) let the UI agent build about 4,300 lines against an engine that did not yet exist, with no engine bugs found at integration.
- **Shared-tree hazards** (L3 §3.4, L4 §4.1): `git add` stages another agent's half-finished hunk; `--amend` swallows their files; `reset` orphans their commit; one agent per worktree outside any synced folder; `main` is an integration tree nobody works in, landing with `merge --ff-only`; gate the commit, not the working tree ("81 green" in the tree while the commit alone died on the first `legalActions`). `preview_start` serves the primary checkout's config whatever directory you are in.
- **Token economy**: do not re-read what you know, read large files by range or grep, `--quiet` everything, tools print findings not material, never view full-size renders in the main context (contact sheets), do not spawn agents for what one agent can do; if a helper underperforms fix its brief and rerun rather than taking over.
- **Measure instead of arguing** (luminance sampling, pixel geometry, arena counts each ended a debate). A user's diagnosis is a symptom report, not a root cause; also say when the reported thing is not a bug. Comment the WHY on every weight, penalty and non-obvious branch.
- **Do not build a balance instrument for a reproduction.** RIVALFORGE (original design) needed it; for a reproduction the arena is a behaviour check and AI tuner, never a reason to change a printed number. Know which regime you are in before spending a day (L5 §6.2). Mancermundo is a reproduction.

## 3. Engine and data patterns that transfer
- **One door per rule; extract on the first duplication.** The most repeated engine bug in the series (damage written to the object in 11 places skipping prevention; costs read in 5 places; attach legality in 4; target pools sliced per pack skipping protection). **A convention a tool can check is a rule; a convention in a doc is a suggestion** - add a grep to the gate the first time a bypass costs an hour (it paid off 4+ times; strip comments first or the gate fails on its own docs). For a skirmish: one damage/wound door, one movement door, one LoS/cover door, one stat door (`charsOf` / `query.*`) with an explicit evaluation order for continuous effects designed on day one.
- **Every UI affordance derives from `legalActions`**; every number the UI shows comes from an engine function. The drag path and the button path cannot disagree because both are filters over the same set.
- **Never assume a choice** - not a target, not an order, not a path. The choice door has no auto-take path (one option or fifty, the player is asked); exceptions are "this one", "all of", indistinguishable objects and zero-option windows. Test it with a single-target case, because every other test passes either way. A solver may pre-select a suggestion the player confirms in one click.
- **Validation that refuses to run:** content validated at load (unknown ids, types, triggers, ops, duplicates throw); an ability the grammar cannot express is `{unimplemented: 'why'}` and validation rejects it from any registered roster - "the single biggest quality lever in the series and it cost nothing"; a skeleton default a real value would replace must fail validation when it survives to play; a table keyed by content (art, sound flavour, keyword help) fails when content is added without an entry.
- **No silent fallbacks:** neither `if (!NS.x)` nor `NS.x || {}` in UI code; a missing module throws on the first frame. Silent fallbacks shipped: abilities not rendered for two days, the AI never loaded (opponent played random legal moves, difficulty selector did nothing), 59 paid renders replaced by placeholders.
- **Write turn history into state from day one** (what each unit did this activation/turn); reconstructing from the log is a bug farm. A zone/status change makes a new object with a new id so stale targets fail naturally.
- **Simultaneous triggers are ordered by their controller as a choice** (active player first); push order was wrong and blocked a card. State-based checks (death, removal) run before anyone acts, repeated until none apply, then triggers.
- **Structured log** `{turn, phase, step, message, data}` read by the log panel, audio and AI alike; one `log()` entry point stamping ids and `via` for triggered consequences (an un-attributed chain read as "the AI took three turns" and was reported as cheating). Gate: every engine log type has a player-facing line, every question kind has a prompt, every mapped sound has a voice. A card paid for that does nothing says so (fizzle notice).
- **Black box before the first playtest:** every match records seed, roster ids, difficulty, every action; a "Flag a bug" button pins the action index; `replay-report --selftest` reports ILLEGAL / THREW / DIVERGED; a roster override must be carried in the trace or replays diverge. Lorcana shipped a "Save bug report" button and `LC.makeTrace/LC.replay`.
- **Hidden information (Dead of Winter, applies to fog or hidden orders):** deciders only receive `view(state, seat)`; a leak test runs inside the sim on every game and must play every card that touches hidden information; the end screen can reveal every hidden decision so the human can audit the bot.
- **Undo only while nothing hidden was revealed** by the undone answer. **Auto-pass windows** when the player holds nothing playable, with a toggle to force every window.
- **Show the partly-resolved state while asking** (Lorcana): keep the paid costs on screen with a snapshot for the replay; showing the pre-job state hid paid costs.
- **Rules-engine traps in general:** read the rule's timing clause ("if" checked on resolution vs trigger); damage-since-last-check records must be cleared after each state check; whole stacks move together; "banish all" moves the group first, then triggers see the group; data feeds' keyword arrays miss keywords, read keyword lines from the text (Lorcast missed Alert). Lorcana-specific citations are in `lorecannae\kb\LORCANA-LESSONS.md`.

## 4. AI
- **Score every candidate against the do-nothing baseline at the same horizon; `pass` is the fallback, not a candidate.** Passing resolves end-of-turn effects (scoring, draw, untap) - a windfall no single action matches. The AI held an objective 18-0 with 7 resource and passed; empty turns fell 9% to 2% after the fix. It reappeared in GRAND LINE exactly as predicted (L5 §5.1, L6 §1).
- **Measure behaviour, not only win rate.** Day-one counters: fraction of turns/activations with zero actions, reactive windows declined that it could use, plus game-specific ones (a zero count for a signature mechanic = a roster the AI cannot play). Win rate is blind when both sides share a bug. Build the counters *with* the AI.
- **An evaluator blind to a rule measures itself:** a new mechanic measured 19.6% to 19.7% until the evaluator got a term (35.9%); 0 block windows in 32 games until a Blocker term (0 to 59, empty turns 5.5% to 0%). Before measuring a rule ask whether the evaluator can perceive it. (Fell-in's bot: normal vs random 24 wins / 0 losses / 6 draws with 12 fallbacks until objective-aware scoring and unhandled decision kinds were fixed, then 29-1-0 over 30 games; objective-blind bots draw.)
- **One extra ply of search beat four argued weight terms** (62.1% vs about 50%). Lorcana: a rollout of the bot's turn plus the opponent's reply on determinized copies beat one-ply play 80%; weight sweeps measured nothing. **Common random numbers are mandatory** when comparing rollout candidates (every candidate gets the same reshuffled worlds): before the fix Hard skipped early inks (3.7% empty turns), after 0.6% and +13 points head-to-head.
- **Ties are where stupid moves come from** (a fifth of play decided by list order). Only systematic choices that are right beat random; a "sensible" tiebreaker can measure worse than random.
- **Measure honestly:** play every pairing in both seats and report only decisive results; hold every change to a holdout seed set (a "56.7%" weight change was 45% on fresh seeds); a peak on a proxy metric can measure exactly 50.0% head-to-head; commit negative results ("do not re-run this sweep"); a fix that changes nothing is information (the problem is structural); run sweeps in a sandbox copy of the repo (in-place sweeps corrupted concurrent test runs); never benchmark against a tree someone else is fixing; bench on a fixed pairing plus the main matchup; a compounding advantage cannot be fixed by a one-off correction.
- **Random play is the control for roster spread**; a random-play fuzzer is not a substitute for testing the AI - two harnesses, both before a human sees it. A bot that is a test instrument has a win rate that is not a difficulty claim.
- The bot waits for presentation to be idle, with a watchdog (about 5 s without progress) that force-answers; the AI must be loaded in the live page (it was not for days and the suite stayed green).
- Searching: cap/sample `legalActions` with deterministic even-stride sampling (never front truncation); prune combinatorial search; cap turns in the arena and report capped games separately.

## 5. UI and presentation (principles; layout details were card-specific)
- **Open information is a rule.** Before the first playable, walk the rules' zone list and give every public zone a viewer: counts for every zone on both sides; any face-up card anywhere hover-zooms; everything revealed, discarded or used as a cost is named in the log and findable afterwards. The hover zoom is one delegated document listener keyed on an id attribute (opt-in per node meant every later modal had none). For a skirmish: every unit's full stat card and active effects on hover, the opponent's remaining actions/ammo/status visible, dead units still inspectable.
- **Every prompt says what it is about**: the source, the target, the numbers, what declining does; numbers on the buttons; "OK" is never a label. Never draw a result behind the prompt that asks about it; hovering an option highlights the exact object it means; read prompt context from state, not the newest log event. Say why an action is unavailable, next to the control, before the first click (`whyNot`).
- **Targeting is a pending choice on state** with `min`/`max` answered by a legal action, so the AI answers through the same path and undo/replay work mid-prompt; the choice modal can be peeked past without answering ("Hide - check the board" plus a floating pill) - "the feature everyone forgets". Decline appears only when `min === 0`. Build the one targeting door before the first ability pack: the targeting retrofit happened twice, each from player reports of cards that "did nothing".
- **Pacing is a feature:** hand-overs hold a beat (about 2.4 s phase / 1.6 s turn), any click skips, a timer always bounds; the opponent's turn plays at a speed a person can follow; spotlight the card the opponent just played, held long enough to read (travel 500 ms, hold 1200, fade 250).
- **Build the animation layer** - deferred by five or six projects running (the lessons files count it differently), closed in about an hour when someone decided to. Separate presented state from live state; a director plays each batch of log events in order; the plan is pure and headless-tested, play happens on the OLD board so a defeated unit is still there to die; input locked during playback with click to skip; speed setting full / quick / instant.
- **Layout is a headless geometry test across real viewport sizes** (zones never overlap, nothing escapes, size floors hold) and the new assertion must fail on the old code first; a green layout test once encoded the bug through five owner reports. Never let a layout input depend on content that changes mid-game (prompt height resized the board); overlays may cover things, layout inputs may not breathe. One redraw path that always repaints roles and re-wires interaction ("drag stops working in Main 1 but not Main 2" looked like a rules bug).
- **Browser traps still relevant to the HTML overlay of a 3D game:** every decorative layer `pointer-events: none` by default (three overlays ate clicks at once); tap vs drag by distance (8 px) never time; suppress the synthetic click after a drop by timestamp AND a capture-phase swallow; `touch-action: none` on draggables; `dvh` not `vh`; `requestAnimationFrame` does not fire in a hidden tab (flush with a forced layout read); `em` cascades from font-size not width; a CSS `background` shorthand wipes `background-image`.
- **The board is the whole window**; everything else is a title screen, an overlaying drawer, or a centred panel; opening a drawer never moves the board; no scrollbars (fix overflow by making content fit). The log is a drawer whose names are hover doorways; a rolling feed of the last few events sits under the prompt, and a gate fails any logged event with no player-facing line.
- **Colour roles from `legalActions` only:** actable (pulse), selected, target, picked; actable is marked, destinations are not; a shared animation phase so redraws do not restart pulses. A faction-coloured border reads as "playable" - identity goes in pips/words, borders carry interaction. Unaffordable/unavailable items dimmed only when the engine confirms the reason.
- **Deck/roster choice is a screen with peek**, "Surprise me", and a pre-picked default so Start is never greyed; deck provenance (URL, fetch date) shown; inferred starter rosters labelled `inferred`.
- **How-to-play, a briefing, keyword help and a lore/objective tracker belong in the first playable** (Fell-in owner: "feels very unfriendly"; Lorcana shipped a guided tutorial, hover previews with keyword help, and a lore track from the first build).

## 6. Audio
- One module owns every audio decision; the audio layer rides the structured log; unknown tags are ignored so the engine may tag sounds that do not exist yet; dedupe per batch.
- **Voice the sound at the moment of the picture, not at apply time; rate-limit frequent tags** (max concurrent, min retrigger gap). A "siren" fired on every payment and drove the player out of the room. SFX were "a day's work and the single largest perceived quality gain" - do them early.
- One `AudioContext` for everything (iPadOS parked a mixed `<audio>`/Web Audio context silently; `wake()` on state change, gesture, visibility, poll); a lookahead scheduler on the audio clock, never `setTimeout`, if anything is synthesised. (Mallet later needed the iPhone fix: gesture listeners that stay for the session and retry until the context runs.)
- Measure generated audio (crest factor; RMS across the kit within about +-15%; a sound per distinct source) and **say "not verified by ear"** until the owner listens on an audition page. The owner rejected a generated procedural score ("the music sucks"); real generated music is expected, the procedural kit is the floor. Lorcana's music tiers rise with the highest lore on the table (escalate the soundtrack as the game nears its end).

## 7. Art direction and generation
- **Three constants under any art direction:** no text rendered in an image; no reproduction of a specific official illustration (never an artist, studio, franchise or game name in a prompt); one original rendering style.
- **Style audition before choosing** (one representative subject in about ten styles, owner picks, recorded in PLAN.md, not re-litigated); one `STYLE` constant, byte-identical on every generation (changing one character starts a new set); an **identity table** (one visual clause per named character written once) plus a **per-item scene clause** (each card its own subject and setting - template prompts produced template plates: nine skills got one portrait). A subject with no entry fails the build.
- **Prompt lint that refuses to write the prompts file**, and it must lint the STYLE constant too ("three-quarter" tripped the count rule: 160 violations before 147 paid renders). Banned: text-magnet nouns (sign, banner, logo, emblem, book, map, screen, magazine cover), negations (diffusion reads the noun), counts above two, franchise/artist/studio names, castle words on UI plates (trade dress), prompts over 900 characters. A proper noun in a clause invites lettering; a vague noun is filled by the model's prior ("a hulking shape" drew a naked man); "cadaverous" or "hollow" draws a skull. Front-load the proportion anchor right after the subject.
- **Sample three, look, then batch.** Idempotent generation (`--dry-run` makes zero network calls and prints the plan, `--only` exact match, `--force` archives first, `--limit` caps a paid run, 429 back-off). **Wire every asset pipeline empty and failing loudly before filling it** (declare the manifest while it is `{}`), and build a self-diagnostic with the feature (`check-art.mjs`: presence, zero length, magic bytes, truncation, case, git-tracked, plus an in-app Art check screen).
- **QC with contact sheets of 16 and a checklist**; borderline tiles get one native-size look; regenerate only failures and archive first (regeneration is not monotonic); crop the bottom 4-8% (the model signs its work). Painterly art compresses worse than flat/cel art (about 140 KB per 560 px WebP at q88; Lorcana's 136 cards were 19 MB until the flat style brought them to 6.2 MB).
- **IP check on a contact sheet** caught designs that drifted toward famous ones even with an identity table (gills read as big ears; a castle title evoked a studio logo): ban the words, re-roll, look again.
- **Plan the asset list from the data at M1, not from the box contents** (11 of 31 units were still procedural after an art batch planned from the wrong list); wire approved assets promptly (the owner asked why placeholders still showed after approving the gate).
- Masters never enter the repo; commit delivery encodes once, after QC; decide the format before the first asset commit (353 MB became 41 MB). Cache-bust deployed static files with `?v=<content hash>` and `--check` it in CI (Pages caches each file ten minutes: a verified fix rendered broken for a returning player).

## 8. Testing and verification
- **Verify in the real browser on the real code path**; every UI commit message says what was checked. "Green tests plus a broken page is the most common failure in this series" (a missing script, an unloaded AI, an `em` base, overflow). Read the DOM when screenshots are unavailable; several "bugs" were test artifacts.
- **One behaviour test per content item in a registered roster**: put it on a board, play through `apply()`, assert the side effect not the resolution. 22 of the first 78 failed on a build whose suite, auditor and page gate were green; 8 were engine bugs touching every deck; MythosForge found 7, MANAFORGE 13, GRAND LINE 8. For Necromunda: one behaviour test per weapon trait, per skill and per special rule that is in a registered gang.
- **Prove a test bites** by reverting the fix (the first banish-credit test passed on buggy code because the trigger had no target); pair every negative with a positive; when a report contradicts a green test, suspect the test; test names state the rule, not the current behaviour.
- **The printed-vs-generated auditor** (diff by id, offline) is the only automated check that catches a clause silently dropped for want of a primitive: first runs found 65, 7 and 7 real defects on green builds. Severity split: FAIL = a printed number/keyword/zone absent from the compiled ability, WARN = wording; "an auditor that cries wolf is worse than no auditor"; explain false positives with an `audit:` note instead of loosening; after loosening a gate prove it still fails on a known-bad case.
- **Headless sim with invariants after every step** (no NaN, no negative resource, phase order, every pending decision has a legal accepted answer, every game ends) plus behaviour counters ("never happens" counters found a rule implemented too permissively: 84/96 games capped with zero combat). A fast full-game browser test is the one browser test worth keeping. All gates runnable in one command printing a few lines (`tools/gate.mjs`, 11 steps in MythosForge).
- **Gate the commit, not the working tree**; CI on push runs the headless suite plus script-list and asset-stamp checks.

## 9. IP and rights (series-wide practice)
- Day one, before code: read the rights holders' published policies from primary pages, record date and URL in `docs/rights.md` / `docs/sources.md`; "searched on <date>, found none" is itself a finding; state the concern **once**, in the file that holds it, then build what was asked in full (never a quietly narrowed version). Fixed limits: no official art/audio in the repo, nothing sold or monetised, nothing presented as official, no circumvention of technical measures.
- `NOTICE.md` names the published game once, states non-affiliation and non-commercial, credits third-party audio, gives a takedown pointer, and the disclaimer also renders on a screen the player sees. `docs/takedown.md` has real commands. Deployment status lives in `PLAN.md` only (four projects drifted: NOTICE, CLAUDE and takedown still said "private" after the owner flipped to public). The stale-notice rule: the commit that changes deployment also rewrites takedown.
- Check the working title and faction names against existing licensed products before they reach a filename, URL or repo name (a rename touched eight files, a repo and a non-redirecting URL). Rights holder postures differ: Disney had the Pixelborn simulator shut down in 2024; Duels.ink runs under the publisher's fan-content policy; Riot's digital-tools policy prohibits rules-enforcing clients; Wizards' fan policy does not cover one; the Asmodee family restricts online versions. (Games Workshop's position is not covered by any card-game file as far as the digests show; the 3D family uses the "Mallet posture" in [README.md](README.md).)

## 10. What to apply to a Necromunda skirmish overnight (card series -> 3D)
1. Playable first: a clickable board with two small gangs vs a random bot before any art, sound or third gang; the owner's playtest then drives the rules.
2. `.gitignore` and the privacy pre-commit hook first; rights findings recorded; PLAN.md owns decisions; RULING log for ambiguities.
3. Freeze the engine contract in a doc before the UI starts, then build UI and engine in parallel.
4. One door per rule with grep gates in the gate command; never assume a choice; every number from `query.*`.
5. Prompts that say what they are about, odds shown, a whyNot for every disabled action, a briefing and keyword hover in the first playable.
6. AI: score against do-nothing, measure zero-action activations and objective play, fixed-pairing bench both seats, holdout seeds, then one reply-ply if time allows.
7. Presentation director with presented-state cursor and bot idle wait from the first client stage; sounds at the picture, rate-limited.
8. One behaviour test per weapon trait / skill / special rule in a registered gang; independent read-only reviewers as soon as content lands.
9. Art: audition, one STYLE constant, identity table per fighter archetype, lint before GPU time, sample three, contact sheets, per-item reference gate.
10. Say "not verified by ear" and "not play-verified by the owner" in the handoff; write the morning summary at the top.
