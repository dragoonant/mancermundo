# 40 - AI opponent (Necromunda)

The AI is the opponent of the solo game: a believable underhive gang that shoots from cover, closes in through doors, fights when the odds favour it, helps its wounded, and does not sit still. One `Decider` per tier, all in `src/ai/`, running in a Web Worker in the client (`src/ai/worker.ts`) or inline (`createAiBot`, the bench, the tests). It sees only `view(state, seat)` and answers only from `pending.options` (finite decisions) or from actions that pass `validate` (continuous ones). Every number it weighs comes from the engine (`query.*`, `dice.ts`, `wargear.ts`, `nav`, `movement`); it composes them, it never re-derives a rule. The Kill Team version of this file is kept in `kill-team-reference/40-ai.md`; the method (per-activation utility scoring, threat map, Web Worker, tiers, bench) is the same, the rules underneath are not.

## 1. Tiers and entry points

| Tier | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `random` | uniform over legal actions (an action type first, so a door option is not drowned by eighteen turn facings); move paths and deploy spots are random legal points. Test instrument and bench baseline. |
| `easy` | the same scoring with noise (a random pick among the best three that are within 0.6 of the best), 10 candidate spots instead of 22, a weaker threat map (x0.45), no second-action lookahead, no scenario objectives. |
| `normal` | full scoring: 22 candidate spots, second-action lookahead (move then shoot, shoot then slip away or take cover), scenario objectives (caskets, doors that open the way), group activation. |

Entry points (`src/ai/index.ts`):

- `createAiDecider(tier, { seed, budgetMs?, weights?, memory?, now? })` implements the engine `Decider`; `decideSync(view, pending, legal)` is the synchronous core and reports `{ action, fallback, ms }`.
- `createAiBot(tier, seed)` is a drop-in for the client's `RandomBot`: `decide(state, pending): Action`, one per seat. The client store switches by replacing `new RandomBot(...)` with `createAiBot(tier, ...)`.
- `AiWorkerClient` (`workerClient.ts`) is a `Decider` over the worker: it posts each decision, and answers in-process with the same code when there is no `Worker`, the worker errors or three seconds pass, so the game always continues. `AiWorkerClient.reset()` at the start of a game clears every decider's memory.
- `handleRequest(req)` is the pure core of the worker: `decide { id, view, pending, legal, tier, seed, memoryKey, budgetMs? } -> action { id, action, ms, fallback } | error { id, message }`, `reset` forgets every decider (a new game). Deciders and their activation memory are kept per `memoryKey` and tier.

All randomness comes from `SeededRng(seed | decision id | tier)`: the same inputs give the same answer. `AI_MS` (default 150) sets the per-decision budget, mapped to a deterministic work cap (candidate spots the planner may evaluate) with a wall-clock hard stop at four times the budget; `AI_TUNE` overrides the weights (`{"threat":0.7}` or `threat=0.7,act=0.15`); `AI_TRACE=1` prints the scored candidates of every `action.choose` (node only).

## 3. The scoring model

Everything is in "ganger units": a Leader is worth 3 (the Tunnel Skirmish points), a Champion 2, anyone else 1, plus 0.45 for a working Heavy weapon and 0.3 for a Special one; a Seriously Injured fighter counts 0.45 (mostly out already), a Broken one 0.8.

**Position value** of a fighter at a spot in a posture: `-threat x exposure + cohesion x supporters - approach x distance to goal (x damp)`. The score of an action is the position value afterwards minus the value now, plus what the action does on the spot; "end the activation" is worth exactly 0. Every other action except Take Cover then gets `act` (0.2, growing through a stalemate), so doing something beats doing nothing unless it costs more than that. Default weights: threat 1, approach 0.09, cohesion 0.07, potential 0.3, act 0.2, pin 0.22, prone 0.18, patience 0.03 (`tiers.ts`; self-play at 16 games per pair could not tell threat 0.5 from 1.3, found approach 0.2 clearly worse than 0.12 and 0.07 a shade better, so the pull is kept modest and the stall breaker does the rest).

- **Exposure (threat map).** For every enemy that is not Seriously Injured: a shot from where the enemy stands now when it has line of sight (range band, cover from the enemy, final need, the wound / save / Injury chain, Prone-in-cover is untargetable), a 0.3 share when it would have to walk into sight, and a charge weighted by the chance that Move + D3 reaches (the enemy's own distance field), using `query.fightOutcome`. Ready enemies count fully, enemies that already acted 0.55, Broken ones 0.15. Probabilities combine as independent events; loss = value x (P(out) + 0.65 P(seriously injured)) + a pin cost. A Prone fighter in partial or full cover has no ranged exposure, which is what makes Take Cover score.
- **Shots** (`shots.ts`). For each usable ranged profile (and twin Sidearm pairs) `targetInfos` gives eligible targets with band, cover, need and priority class; the value is `query`-grade: hit chance x the hit chain (`hitChain` x `shotOutcome`, checked against `query.expectedOutcome` in `AI-016`) -> expected Out of Action, Seriously Injured, pins and Wounds, minus ammo risk (Firepower Ammo symbol x failed Ammo test), minus stray-shot cost (a friend Engaged with the target or within an inch of the line), with Blast splash on friends counted against it. A farther target that needs a Cool test is priced as `pass x value + (1 - pass) x the closest target`. Blasts at floor points and Templates use the engine's aim suggestions.
- **Melee.** Charge: `pReach x (dealt - extra exposure at the contact spot) - (1 - pReach) x 0.15`; Fight: dealt - 0.5 x threat x blows taken (the enemies' `fightOutcome` against us), with +0.1 so an Engaged fighter fights on ties; Retreat only when staying costs far more than leaving (a hurt fighter against a stronger one); Coup de Grace is worth about 1.8 x the target's value.
- **Moves.** Candidate end spots come from the fighter's own distance field: a half-inch lattice ranked by progress toward the goal and an inch lattice ranked by being seen by the three nearest enemies; the best 22 become paths the engine's `planMove` accepts (straight lines first, the engine's route finder for the few a wall hides). Spots on obstacles, bases or within an inch of an enemy never reach the planner. A move that only undoes the last one (or the first move of this activation) is penalised.
- **Second actions.** Move then shoot scores `0.85 x` the shot from the new spot (facing chosen toward the best target: the `move.path` carries the facing); shoot then slip away or take cover scores the shot plus the best follow-up gain; Aim then Shoot scores the aimed shot; Stand Up then Shoot is a pseudo-move; Take Cover pays for the exposure it removes minus 0.18 for lying down.
- **Goals.** A gunner wants to stand inside 80% of its best Short range (3 to 10"), a brawler wants contact. The pull toward the goal is damped to a quarter while a gunner has a shot in hand, so it shoots instead of marching. When every enemy is behind a closed door the door on the route is the goal (unlocked doors first; a locked one only when nothing else leads there), and `operateDoor` / `forceDoor` / `accessTerminal` score 0.75 to 0.8 when they open that door and are negative otherwise (so they never pass for a filler action).
- **Stall breaker.** `quiet = rounds since anything went down`; after one quiet round the approach pull, the `act` bonus and the disregard for exposure grow by about 24% per round.
- **Who activates.** A cheap value per Ready fighter: its best shot (trying the two nearest facings), the best charge on offer (distance field, not the engine's per-target routes), half the danger it stands in, Broken fighters first (they must run for cover). A Leader or Champion adds 20% of the best values of the fighters within 6" it could take along. **Group** nominations take the best Ready friends worth at least 0.22.
- **Turn.** A free turn is taken first only when it opens a shot that is at least 0.12 better.
- **Finish restraint.** Forgotten Riches and Smash & Grab are won on caskets, yet a battle ends the moment one gang is gone. While the AI is level or behind on secured loot (Forgotten Riches) or has not cracked three caskets as the raider (Smash & Grab) and two or fewer enemies still stand, damage is worth a quarter (Forgotten Riches) or less (`decider.ts` `damped`; x0.5 with seven or more standing, x0.3 with four to six, x0.1 with three or fewer), so it hauls and cracks instead of ending the battle in a draw or a defender win.

**Set-up.** Role: attacker, else deploy second. Crew: Leader, Champions, then the best-armed and toughest. Half or tile: the one with more cover to deploy behind, nearer the loot. Deployment: heavy and special weapons first, the Leader last; each fighter takes the lattice spot where the enemy zone sees it least (walls and cover from five sample points of the opposing zone), with a lane for its gun, a few inches from a friend (not on top of one), facing the enemy; a cramped zone is packed the way the engine does (`suggest`). Casket spots: the engine's.

**End phase and rule grants.** Bottle: take the test; bottle voluntarily only when at most one fighter stands against three or more and the test would bottle anyway at 50% or better. Flight: decline. Assist: the most valuable Seriously Injured fighter first; the Seriously Injured crawl toward a standing friend (an assister) and a safer spot. Recovery pick: the kindest die. `skill.choice`: first offer that is not a decline; `dice.reroll`: take it; `confirm`: concede a locked win, otherwise continue.

**Scenarios.** Smash & Grab attackers walk to the nearest closed casket and crack it (Smash or Bypass, whichever the engine's odds favour); Forgotten Riches gangs go for loot that is not yet on their own tile and Carry it (a Move with the casket) to the nearest comfortable point of their deployment strips, never opening a casket (an ammo cache is no loot). Both read `query.scenarioView`.

## 4. Never random, always counted

The decider answers through `POLICIES[kind]`. A missing policy, a `null`, an answer that is not one of the legal options (or fails `validate` for continuous kinds) or a throw is answered by `suggestPolicy`: the engine's `suggest`, else the first legal option that is not a pass, else a pass; never a random action for `easy` and `normal`. Each use is counted: `stats.fallbacks` (and per kind), `stats.unhandled[kind]` for kinds with no policy at all, `stats.errors` for failures, `stats.hardStops` when the wall-clock stop fired. The bench fails on any of them. `POLICIES` is typed `Record<DecisionKind, Policy>`, so adding a decision kind to the contract does not compile until it has a policy (`AI-001`).

## 5. Bench (`npm run bench:ai`)

`npm run bench:ai -- --games 40 [--seed 1] [--start 0] [--tier normal] [--vs random|easy|normal] [--scenario all|scn.tunnel-skirmish|scn.smash-and-grab|scn.forgotten-riches] [--rounds 12] [--weights threat=0.7] [--verbose]`. The tested tier plays Goliath and Escher in turn in both seats (game g: the AI is Goliath for `g mod 4 < 2`, seat A for even g), against the opponent tier playing the other house, rotating through the starter gangs, the three scenarios (every four games) and the shipped boards. The result is the engine's. A battle that reaches the round cap (`--rounds`, default 12) with equal scenario points is decided by the scenario's own projection (caskets secured or opened) and then by the casualty-weighted margin; those are counted separately (`decidedByCasualtyMargin`).

Gates (normal vs random): decisive win rate >= 95%; zero rejections (either seat), stalls, fallbacks, unhandled decision kinds and policy errors; activations that ended with no action while one was on offer < 2%; decision p50 < 50 ms. The report (`tools/out/bench-report.json`) also lists wins by scenario and house, the action mix, the zero-action activations by status, the decision kinds seen, decision p50 / p95 / max and per-kind timings.

Recorded run (S6, `npm run bench:ai -- --games 40`, seed 1, all three scenarios): normal vs random 39 wins, 1 loss (a Smash & Grab raid by Escher that cracked two of the three caskets before the defenders' gang broke and the battle ended), 0 draws; 0 rejections, stalls, fallbacks, unhandled kinds or policy errors; 0.3% of 1968 activations with no action; decision p50 0.9 ms, p95 16.4 ms, mean 4.2 ms, max 59 ms (inline, node, structured-clone safe); Smash & Grab raids 8 of 9 won (18 caskets cracked), Forgotten Riches 12 of 12 won (5 caskets secured against 0). A 90-game sweep (seed 31) gave 86 wins, 2 losses (both Smash & Grab raids of the same kind), 2 draws, 0 rejections; the 90-game sweeps on two other seeds had 0 rejections as well. Normal beats easy 18 to 2 with 4 draws (24 games, seed 5); easy beats random 22 to 1 with 1 draw. The numbers move with the weights; the bench is the authority.

## 6. Known weaknesses and non-goals

- No multi-round lookahead and no opponent modelling: it reacts to the board it sees. It does not bait, hold fighters back for a counter-charge, or time its Leader's group activation.
- Stand-offs: two cautious gangs facing each other through a door hold until the stall breaker raises the pressure (about two quiet rounds); it will walk into a lane eventually, not elegantly.
- Blasts are valued but fighters still bunch at doors, so a human with a launcher punishes clusters.
- Ductways (Crawl, Fire Through) are valued only by the shot they offer; the AI does not route a gang through one.
- Forgotten Riches: loot hauling is plain (nearest unsecured casket, straight home); it does not steal from the enemy tile on purpose or guard its own, and a Seriously Injured fighter next to loot is ignored.
- Set-up is a one-fighter-at-a-time heuristic, not a game-theoretic placement.
- Found while benching, not in the AI: the engine can dead-end Forgotten Riches tile deployment (a strip too narrow once earlier placements are made leaves a fighter with no legal spot and `setup.deploy` cannot be passed); the AI packs a cramped strip with `suggest`, the random tier does the same, but a human placing badly can still hit it. A battle that ends with equal loot is a draw, so an early rout of the other gang (which a random-crew opponent invites) draws Forgotten Riches.
- A `hard` tier (two-activation lookahead, opponent modelling) is the later step; the planner's `lookahead` switch and the per-decision work budget are where it would plug in.
